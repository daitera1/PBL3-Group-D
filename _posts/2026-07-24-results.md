## Final Outcome

CarePath Navigator progressed from an early smart-city healthcare concept into a deployed web prototype supported by user research, system architecture, and a machine-learning evaluation.

The final evaluated scope uses **Hong Kong public-hospital waiting-time data**. NHS and UK sources were investigated during the proposal stage as examples of how a future live-data integration could work, but they were not used for the final reported model evaluation.

[Open the CarePath Navigator prototype](https://route-to-care-ai.lovable.app)

## Machine-Learning Evaluation

The team used an **XGBoost regression model** to estimate hospital waiting times from historical data. Performance was measured on held-out data using three regression metrics.

| Metric | Result | Meaning |
|---|---:|---|
| Mean Absolute Error | **5.93 min** | Average absolute difference between predicted and observed waits |
| Root Mean Squared Error | **8.42 min** | Error measure that gives additional weight to larger misses |
| R² | **0.8105** | Proportion of variation explained on the held-out evaluation |

### Example Prototype Output

| Output | Value |
|---|---:|
| Predicted waiting time | **35.3 minutes** |
| Confidence | **83.1%** |
| Displayed uncertainty | **±6.0 minutes** |

These values demonstrate the final team evaluation. The deployed interface uses prepared model outputs to demonstrate the user experience; it is not connected to a continuously retraining production service.

## User Research

The team surveyed **89 participants** from Japanese, Filipino, American, and other backgrounds.

| Finding | Result |
|---|---:|
| Participants who had experienced long hospital waits | **83%** |
| Interest in real-time or predicted waiting times | **82%** |
| Interest in hospital reviews | **71%** |
| Interest in language-support information | **69%** |

The team also interviewed healthcare professionals. Their feedback reinforced the need to show uncertainty, avoid presenting the application as diagnostic, and keep recommendations understandable.

## What We Delivered

- A deployed hospital-comparison web prototype.
- Fastest-versus-closest recommendation controls.
- Predicted wait-time and confidence presentation.
- Hospital information and navigation flow.
- Requirements specification and system architecture.
- Class, component, container, deployment, sequence, state, system-context, pipeline, and use-case diagrams.
- User survey and expert-interview findings.
- Final presentation and demonstration.

## Limitations

- The prototype is not a medical, diagnostic, or emergency-care tool.
- The final interface demonstrates prepared prediction outputs rather than a live hospital API.
- Prediction quality depends on the freshness, coverage, and reliability of source data.
- Results should be validated with a larger time range and controlled deployment before real-world use.
- Language, insurance, review, and crowdsourced reporting features require further verification and privacy design.

## Next Steps

1. Establish an authorized live-data partnership.
2. Automate data validation, freshness monitoring, and model retraining.
3. Conduct accessibility and usability testing with patients and caregivers.
4. Test recommendation safety, confidence thresholds, and failure states.
5. Expand only after the Hong Kong implementation is validated.
